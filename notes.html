Process

How it was built

Prompts were drafted with Claude in a separate chat, then run and refined in Claude Code.

Decisions

Key decisions

Who drove it: Me (the person building Side Control) Claude chat Claude Code

  1. The problem came from the mats, not a list

    Me Overruled AI suggestions

    What changed
    Claude suggested four project ideas. I chose my own instead: booking private BJJ lessons. A nutrition-tracking idea was dropped because that market is already crowded.
    Why
    It's a problem I've seen first-hand, so the brief could be specific.

  2. Invented coach, academy and students

    Me

    What changed
    No real academy or instructor appears on the page or in any prompt. The demo uses an invented coach and academy, and the academy name was checked so it doesn't match a real club.
    Why
    Real people's names and businesses don't belong in a concept demo.

  3. Research changed the pitch

    Claude chat

    What changed
    The starting assumption was “gym software doesn't do private lessons”. Checking at the source showed Gymdesk does support bookable private sessions. The pitch moved from “nobody has built this” to “the gym software belongs to the club; coaches still run private lessons over WhatsApp from their phone”.
    Why
    It's more honest, and a stronger story. The second alternative, BJJNote, covers notes but not booking.

  4. Price shown up front and locked

    Me

    What changed
    Two requirements were added: the student sees their own rate before booking, the rate is locked to the booking, and the coach marks it paid as Cash or Revolut.
    Why
    Private lessons are usually agreed verbally, rates vary by student, and that leads to an awkward “I thought it was €X” moment at the end of a session.

  5. The name: Side Control, with Next Stripe for students

    Me Overruled an AI suggestion

    What changed
    Another AI tool suggested “FirstRoll: The Private Coaching Platform”. Claude chat pushed back: a first roll is a beginner's first spar, so the name speaks to the student, not the coach who pays, and “platform” oversells a demo. After a brainstorm, I picked Side Control (the position, the side hustle, and control of the diary) and Next Stripe for the student view.
    Why
    The buyer is the coach. The name had to speak to them.

  6. Independence, never concealment

    Me Claude chat

    What changed
    I raised that a coach may not want their private lessons running through the club's system. Claude chat refined it: the point is whose account and data it is, not cost. The pitch became “your diary, your rates, your students' notes”, and the target widened to any black or brown belt teaching private lessons on the side.
    Why
    It's a tool for the coach's own business. It's never framed as hiding income from the club.

  7. Treated as a real product, honestly labelled

    Me

    What changed
    The page discloses its status the way a start-up would (“Concept product. Demo runs on invented sample data.”). A “Built with AI” footer line was removed and replaced by this page.
    Why
    One line undersold the human direction behind the build. This page tells the full story.

  8. Students don't WhatsApp the coach

    Me Overruled an AI suggestion

    What changed
    WhatsApp is in the demo, but only started by the coach: pre-written click-to-chat messages with no phone numbers stored. The plan had students messaging the coach after booking; I cut that and replaced it with an in-app notification bell.
    Why
    A coach with a dozen private students would be swamped. Bookings should land in the app, not in another chat.

  9. Late-cancel fees, “Oss” and “Tap out”

    Me Claude Code

    What changed
    I added “Oss” (confirm) and “Tap out” (cancel), late-cancel fees the coach switches on, student stats and a coach-only “Needs a nudge” flag. Claude Code made the detailed calls: the tap-out that reaches the threshold carries the fee, coach cancellations never charge a student, and “Pay upfront required” stays in the coach's view so the student never sees the flag.
    Why
    Fewer lost sessions, and a money reason for a coach to pay for the tool.

  10. From belt blue to a black belt colour scheme

    Me Overruled the original AI-chosen colour

    What changed
    The first design used a belt-blue accent. After a coach in testing called it “a bit insulting” for an app aimed at black belts, I switched the brand to black, the red bar and white stripes. Claude Code moved every status (Tomorrow, Late cancel, Unpaid…) to colours that can't be confused with the brand, each with an icon and a text label. Students still show their real belt colours.
    Why
    The brand has to respect the people who'd pay for it, and statuses must work without colour.

  11. Packs, homework and a monthly money view

    Me Claude Code

    What changed
    Requested in testing by a club owner: packs of 10 or 20 sessions, homework and a video link in the notes, and a monthly money summary with a CSV download. Claude Code decided how each figure is dated and checked the totals against an independent count.
    Why
    That's how private lessons are often sold and tracked in practice.

  12. Private notes, public summary

    Me

    What changed
    The day-to-day build log is kept outside the public code. This page is the polished public version, with private details removed.
    Why
    Working notes include things that don't belong on a public page.

Testing

What testing found, and what we changed

  1. The demo was blank on a real iPhone

    The first real-phone check found the demo worked on a laptop but showed nothing on an iPhone. Safari had kept an old copy of the page's files.

    Changed: version numbers on the page's files so phones always fetch the latest, code rules that avoid features older iPhone Safari lacks, and an on-screen error message if the demo ever fails to start.

    Lesson: AI testing on a laptop isn't the same as a real person on a real phone.

  2. Who tried it, and what they tried

    A club owner who teaches private lessons, another coach, and two students testing at the same time. They booked as students, used the coach view, and had two students book the same slot at once in separate browsers.

  3. What broke: a double booking

    Two students both booked and confirmed the same slot.

    Changed: before any booking or confirmation is saved, the demo re-reads the latest data and checks the slot is still free; if it's gone, the student sees “Sorry, that slot was just taken” with the next free times. Other open tabs update by themselves.

    Being honest about the limit: two different browsers or phones keep separate data, so they can't see each other yet. That needs a shared server Planned, and the requirements table now says so.

  4. What confused them, and what we changed

    • No tutorial: a dismissible “Try this” hint strip at the top of the demo.
    • How students get access: a “How students get in” section, and a note on the demo's student picker.
    • The blue accent: a black belt colour scheme.
    • Where data is stored: a “Your data” section, with the launch plan clearly marked Planned.
    • No monthly view of money: a monthly summary with a CSV download.

  5. Asked for in testing

    Built: packs of 10 or 20 sessions with sessions remaining, homework and video links in the notes, and the monthly money summary. Now Planned: shared private lessons for 2 or 3 students, and a Brazilian Portuguese version.

  6. A real iPhone test, round two

    A real iPhone test after the content round found the logo got lost on the dark header, and packs and the monthly summary were hard to find. The logo now sits on a light chip, and both features are on the first screens you see. Next: re-check on the iPhone at version 9.

Honesty check

Honesty check of the main page

Every claim on the main page was checked and sorted into one of three groups. Anything that fitted none of them was fixed.

  • True of the demo

    The hero promise and the “Concept product” line; the three steps; “How students get in”; all six requirement rows; the demo's text, hint strip and labels; “What we changed” in testing; “This demo” in Your data; the waiting-list note that nothing is sent or saved; the footer.

  • Labelled Planned

    The club-wide version; cross-phone booking sync (shared server); one-tap sign-in links; the full “Coming later” list; the “When it launches” data plan; coach sign-up.

  • Labelled assumption

    The €9 a month price, the first month free, and the reasoning behind them; who it's for; how coaches cope today (from our own experience, not a survey).

  • Also labelled

    The four problem quotes are marked “Illustrative quotes, invented”. The two alternatives are sourced, linked and marked “Checked 27 Oct 2026”. “Our view” is labelled as an opinion.

  • Fixed during the check

    • Step 2 said nobody else could take a booked slot. That's now qualified: it works between tabs on one device, and across phones needs the shared server (Planned).
    • The waiting list said “we'll let you know”, but the form saves nothing. It now says sign-up isn't open yet (Planned).
    • “Who it's for” and “How coaches cope today” are now labelled as assumptions.
    • “We tested it” was a placeholder. It's now filled in.

  • Confirmed

    • No testimonials, ratings, customer counts or statistics presented as real.
    • No logos. Gymdesk and BJJNote are named as text for comparison. WhatsApp and Revolut are named as plain text because the product works with them. The footer says all four are not affiliated.
    • No real personal data: every coach, academy, student, price and booking in the demo is invented.
    • The waiting-list form sends nothing: it has no destination, and submitting only shows a thank-you.
